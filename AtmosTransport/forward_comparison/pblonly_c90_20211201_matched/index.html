Boundary-layer mixing only: matching AtmosTransport to GCHP, step by step

GEOS-IT meteorology · C90 cubed sphere, native L72 · 2021-12-01 00–24 UTC · page generated 2026-10-15 20:45 UTC (each figure carries its own "Made" time) · earlier steps: first comparison, matched air mass

In short. GCHP 14.7.0 is the reference. To make AtmosTransport mix the boundary layer the way GCHP does, AtmosTransport needed four changes (two new preprocessing options, two code fixes) and two settings; GCHP itself was left at its defaults except that its time step was shortened from 600 s to 240 s, because AtmosTransport cannot step at 600 s. With all changes the cell-by-cell difference of the lowest-layer tracer fell from 0.015–0.027 to 0.002–0.006 mol/mol, and the fraction of tracer left in the lowest layer agrees to within +0.15–0.3 % (it was off by −0.35 to −1.4 %).

The experiment and why we do it

  • Question: given identical meteorology, grid and initial tracer, do AtmosTransport and GCHP mix a surface tracer up through the boundary layer at the same rate? If not, which modelling choices cause the difference? This is the first of a series that compares one transport process at a time, so that each difference can be traced to its cause before processes are combined.
  • What is on and off: only boundary-layer (PBL) mixing, GEOS-Chem's non-local scheme in both models. Advection, convection, chemistry, emissions and deposition are off in both, so nothing else moves the tracer.
  • Initial condition and period: a CO2 tracer of 1 mol/mol (dry) in the lowest model layer and 0 above, everywhere on the globe, at 2021-12-01 00 UTC; 24 hours, output every hour.
  • Meteorology and grid: GEOS-IT; GCHP reads the C180 files and regrids to C90 itself, AtmosTransport reads a C90 binary preprocessed from the same C180 files; both on the native 72 levels.
  • What agreement looks like: the same fraction of tracer left in the lowest layer at every hour (global mass-weighted), and lowest-layer maps that differ only at the level of numerical noise. A systematic offset in the fraction means one model mixes faster; a large cell-by-cell difference means the models see different inputs or make different local choices.

The reference: GCHP 14.7.0

A standard GCHP 14.7.0 run directory (created with createRunDir.sh, TransportTracers simulation with a CO2 tracer added). Changed from its defaults, for the experiment only:

  • geoschem_config.yml: only pbl_mixing is active (use_non_local_pbl: true); transport, convection, chemistry, dry and wet deposition are switched off; HEMCO EMISSIONS: false.
  • Time step 600 s → 240 s (HEARTBEAT_DT, RUN_DT, … = 240; chemistry step 720 s, unused). This is the only change to how GCHP computes the mixing. The reason is on the AtmosTransport side: its PBL mixing runs once per transport substep, and the number of substeps per hour is set by the flow (18–22 per hour on this day, about 3 min each). It cannot step at GCHP's 600 s, so both models were brought to a common 240 s (15 substeps per hour).

How AtmosTransport had to be modified to match GCHP

All changes are on AtmosTransport's side, in the order they were found. Changes 1–4 are code; the new options are off by default, so existing AtmosTransport runs are unchanged.

#What AtmosTransport did by defaultWhat GCHP doesWhat we changedEffect
1 GEOS-IT binaries carried no latent heat flux, so GEOS-Chem's non-local PBL scheme could not run on GEOS-IT at all. Reads EFLUX from the GEOS-IT A1 files. Code: pass GEOS-IT EFLUX through to the binary (pbl_eflux). Prerequisite; already used in the first comparison.
2 Dry air per layer: each layer loses its own water vapour, Δp_dry = (1 − q) Δp. GEOS-Chem's dry hybrid coordinate: Δp_dry = ΔA + ΔB · p_s,dry; the column's water is removed in proportion to ΔB. New option dry_mass_layers = "geoschem_hybrid" (preprocessing). AtmosTransport's lowest layer held 0.65 % less dry air than GCHP's; now equal to 4e-7.
3 Pinned the global mean dry surface pressure to 98,325 Pa in every window. No global pin: each window follows the met file's surface pressure. Setting [mass_fix] enable = false (preprocessing). Removes a +0.02 % offset in column dry air.
4 The PBL scheme rebuilds each column's moist pressures from the dry air mass, assuming the default split of change 2. With the GEOS-Chem split this made the lowest layer 0.65 % too thick inside the scheme. Uses the met moist pressure edges directly. Code fix: the scheme reads the binary's dry-air split from its header and rebuilds the moist column to match (edges A + B p_s with p_s recovered from the dry column). Moist geometry inside the scheme now equals GCHP's to rounding.
5 Adaptive transport substeps, 18–22 per hour (164–200 s). One step per HEARTBEAT_DT (default 600 s; here 240 s). Setting min_steps_per_window = max_steps_per_window = 15 (240 s), with GCHP at 240 s. Removes the time-step difference; explained the first-hour gap.
6 The scheme's temperature (3-hourly GEOS-IT I3 file, records at 00, 03, … UTC) was held at the record at the start of each 3-hour block, so it was up to 3 h old. Interpolates the I3 temperature linearly in time at every step (ExtData TMPU1/TMPU2; confirmed from GCHP's own 20-minute temperature output). New option vdiff_temperature_time = "window_midpoint": temperature interpolated in time to the middle of each hourly window. The largest single improvement: the cell-by-cell difference fell 3–5×.

The final AtmosTransport preprocessing settings that differ from the default GEOS-IT C90 profile:

[source]
dry_mass_layers        = "geoschem_hybrid"   # change 2 (default "level_humidity")
vdiff_temperature_time = "window_midpoint"   # change 6 (default "block_start")

[numerics]
min_steps_per_window   = 15                  # change 5: 240 s steps (default adaptive, 1..512)
max_steps_per_window   = 15

[mass_fix]
enable                 = false               # change 3 (default: pin to 98,325 Pa)

The AtmosTransport run configuration itself is the same in all stages: advection "none", diffusion "geoschem_nonlocal_vdiff", convection "none", Float64 on GPU. Already the same in both models before any change: all 72 native levels, hourly surface pressure and humidity from the CTM_I1 files, the hourly PBL inputs (PBL height, friction velocity, heat fluxes), and the 3-hourly winds held over each 3-hour block. The changes are on the local AtmosTransport branch feature/gchp-like (commits dcd5bbb1, 944e4824, 64efeba6) and are not yet on GitHub.

What is left, and what was ruled out

  • Remaining: AtmosTransport keeps 0.15–0.3 % more tracer in the lowest layer than GCHP. About half of it is probably the per-step temperature update above. The rest, about 0.1 %, is near the noise and would point to small differences in the two implementations of the scheme (Richardson-number closure, choice of the PBL-top level, tridiagonal solver); a single-column comparison would settle it.
  • Ruled out:
    • timing of the PBL inputs: both use the A1 record stamped HH:30 for HH:00–HH+1:00;
    • PBL height regridding C180 → C90 (4e-4);
    • wind timing: both hold the 3-hourly A3dyn winds over each block;
    • humidity timing (no measurable effect);
    • mixing-ratio basis: GCHP solves on moist layer weights and then rescales each column, AtmosTransport on dry air. The rate difference is 0.01 % and of the wrong sign;
    • GCHP also mixes humidity and then updates mixing ratios, but its dry layer thickness depends only on the dry surface pressure, so the rescale factor is exactly 1.
